Batch and Expiry Records: A Receiving Checklist for Aesthetic Clinic Buyers

Learn how to effectively manage Batch and Expiry Records with our comprehensive receiving checklist for aesthetic clinic buyers.

HANSEONG BEAUTY GLOBAL Editorial Team · November 04, 2026

This article provides a detailed checklist for aesthetic clinic buyers to manage Batch and Expiry Records, ensuring quality control during deliveries.

Understanding Batch and Expiry Records in Aesthetic Clinics

For aesthetic clinics, managing inventory efficiently is crucial to maintaining service quality and patient safety. One of the key components of this management involves the careful handling of Batch and Expiry Records wholesale. These records help track products and ensure that the clinic adheres to safety regulations and best practices, ultimately safeguarding patient health.

Procurement Decisions: Accept or Hold?

When a delivery arrives, the clinic's inventory manager faces a critical decision: to accept or hold the incoming delivery. This decision is primarily based on the verification of batch and expiry records. An effective receiving checklist can significantly aid in this process. It is important to ensure that all products meet the required standards before they are added to the clinic's inventory.

Creating a Receiving Checklist

To streamline the process of accepting or holding deliveries, it is essential to develop a comprehensive receiving checklist. This checklist should separate document checks from physical inspections. Here’s a detailed breakdown:

Document Checks

  • Invoice Verification: Ensure that the invoice matches the order details, including product names, quantities, and prices.
  • Batch Records: Check that all batch numbers are documented and correspond with the shipment details.
  • Expiry Dates: Confirm that all products have valid expiry dates and fall within acceptable limits for use.
  • Certificates of Analysis: Request these documents from the supplier to verify product quality and compliance.

Physical Inspection

  • Product Condition: Inspect the packaging for any signs of damage or tampering.
  • Quantity Check: Physically count the items received to ensure they match the order.
  • Labeling: Verify that all products are correctly labeled with batch numbers and expiry dates.
  • Temperature Control: For temperature-sensitive products, check that they have been stored and transported under appropriate conditions.

Hypothetical Example of a Receiving Scenario

Hypothetical: Imagine your clinic receives a delivery of hyaluronic acid fillers. The invoice states that you ordered 10 units, with batch number HA12345 and an expiry date of 12/2025. Upon checking the documents, you find that the batch number matches, and the expiry date is acceptable. However, during the physical inspection, you notice that one of the boxes is damaged, raising concerns about the integrity of the product.

In this situation, your decision to accept or hold should weigh heavily on the condition of the packaging. If the product inside appears compromised or if there are visible signs of leakage, it may be prudent to hold the delivery until clarifications are obtained from the supplier. You might contact the supplier to inquire about their return policy for damaged goods and request a replacement or refund.

Questions to Ask Your Supplier

Before finalizing any purchase, it’s essential to communicate effectively with your supplier. Here are some questions to consider sending:

  • Can you provide the certificates of analysis for the products?
  • What are the specific storage conditions for these products during transport?
  • Are there any known issues with the batch numbers being shipped?
  • What is your policy for handling damaged goods upon delivery?
  • How often do you conduct quality checks on your products?

Responsibilities of Clinics, Resellers, and Importers

It’s important to distinguish the responsibilities of clinics, resellers, and importers in the procurement process. Clinics must ensure that they have the right checks in place to verify the quality of products received. Resellers should provide accurate documentation and maintain transparency about the products they distribute. Importers are responsible for compliance with regulations and ensuring that products meet safety standards before they reach the clinic.

By following a structured receiving checklist and maintaining open communication with suppliers, aesthetic clinics can ensure they manage their Batch and Expiry Records effectively. This not only helps in compliance but also in providing safe and effective treatments to patients.

Final Thoughts on Batch and Expiry Records Management

Effective management of Batch and Expiry Records is a cornerstone of successful procurement in aesthetic clinics. By implementing a thorough receiving checklist, clinic inventory managers can make informed decisions about accepting or holding deliveries. This structured approach minimizes risks associated with product quality and patient safety, ensuring that your clinic remains compliant and trustworthy in the eyes of your patients.
